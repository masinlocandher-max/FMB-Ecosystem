I work in communications, creative direction, photography, training and community projects. This page is the personal side of my application, who I am, where I come from, and why I want to work with Miss Intercontinental.

Why I want to join the organization ↘

01

Masinloc made me pay attention.

I come from Masinloc, a coastal municipality in Zambales. A lot of the work I care about began with noticing something in my hometown that I did not want to lose.

I started looking closely at local history because I kept finding details repeated for years without anyone checking where they came from. That led me into source checking, cultural documentation and the preservation of Tina Sambal, the language connected to my community.

I prefer practical preservation. A language survives when people use it. History matters when people know it. So I have worked on learning materials, stories and digital resources that make local knowledge easier to reach.

02

Community work is where my ideas are tested.

Service is not one activity to me. Sometimes it is direct outreach. Sometimes it is teaching. Sometimes it is helping someone prepare for work. Sometimes it is doing the research because people deserve a better answer.

“How can what I know become useful to someone other than me?”
What that looks like in practice

Teaching and training. Cultural research. Language preservation. Community-centered digital resources. Practical information made easier to understand and use.

03

Education gave my work structure.

01

Bachelor of Science in Tourism Management

Tourism trained me to think about people, destinations, hospitality, events, culture and the way places present themselves to the world.

02

Graduate studies in History, University of the Philippines Baguio

History strengthened the way I research, compare sources and separate documented facts from stories that still need verification.

03

Educator and professional trainer

Teaching and training taught me to explain clearly, read a room and guide people with different levels of confidence and experience.

Tourism taught me how places are presented. History taught me to ask whether the story being presented is true.

04

I was a beauty queen myself.

I understand pageantry from the candidate side because I have lived it. I was a Miss Gay Zambales 1st Runner-Up, and I know what it feels like to prepare, rehearse, be judged, manage expectations and still show up composed when the lights turn on.

That experience changed the way I look at delegates. Behind every polished appearance is a person handling schedules, wardrobe, interviews, supporters, pressure and exhaustion at the same time.

It is one reason I care about clear delegate communication and respectful coordination. I want the public experience and the backstage experience to work well.

06

Why Miss Intercontinental should hire me

I would not come in asking only what needs to be posted. I would look at how communication, delegate experience, public image, sponsors and operations affect one another.

01

I connect communication and operations.

If a delegate brief is unclear, that becomes an operational problem. If a campaign is weak, that becomes a perception problem. I am used to looking at both.

02

I understand candidates as people.

I have stood on a pageant stage myself. Clear schedules, useful reminders and respectful handling can change the way a delegate experiences an entire competition.

03

I make stories easier to understand.

International delegates should not feel interchangeable. Each woman brings a country, a culture, a personality and a reason people might remember her.

04

I think about sponsor value.

A sponsor should receive something better than a logo on a poster. I can help think through visibility, content, partnerships and how value is communicated back to supporters.

05

I notice what can be improved.

I respect systems that work. I also ask questions when something is unclear, inefficient or easy to strengthen.
