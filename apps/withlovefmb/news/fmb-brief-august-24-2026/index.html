A consequential policy week begins with inflation, oil and climate risk all pointing in the same direction.

The BSP faces another difficult rate decision, the Duterte impeachment trial changes schedule, Washington prepares a new economic offensive against Iran, Philippine founders gather around AI commercialization, and El Niño is becoming a serious food, water and growth risk.

Philippines · Economy + monetary policy

The BSP enters Thursday squeezed between weak growth and stubborn inflation.

Nineteen of 24 analysts surveyed by BusinessWorld expect the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas to raise its target reverse repurchase rate by 25 basis points to 5.00% on August 27. The central bank has already raised rates twice this year as inflation remained above target.

The economic backdrop is unusually uncomfortable. Second-quarter GDP grew only 2.3% year on year, the weakest pace since 2021, while July inflation eased only slightly to 6.2%. Governor Eli Remolona has said the weaker growth allows the BSP to be less aggressive, but that inflation still needs a more convincing downward trend before policy can relax.

FMB read

The rate decision matters, but the statement may matter more. If the BSP signals that oil and peso pressure dominate, businesses should expect financing conditions to stay tight. If growth weakness begins to dominate the language, the hiking cycle may be approaching its limit.

Philippines · Politics + accountability

The Duterte impeachment trial is changing its clock, not its stakes.

The Senate impeachment court has moved Vice President Sara Duterte’s trial back to afternoon hearings after repeated weather disruptions. The House prosecution panel supports later hearings and has proposed adding Fridays to recover lost trial days. Duterte denies the allegations against her, which include misuse of public funds, unexplained wealth and threats against President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. and others.

The proceeding remains consequential beyond the legal record because conviction could remove Duterte from office and bar her from future public office, directly affecting the 2028 presidential race. The prosecution and defense continue to contest both evidence and procedure.

Why it matters

Scheduling sounds procedural, but delay itself affects legitimacy. A trial this politically important has to be both thorough and visibly functional. Repeated postponements risk turning process into narrative, especially when every side already claims institutional bias.

Middle East · Energy + geopolitics

The U.S. is escalating the economic war on Iran while Hormuz remains the pressure point.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is expected to announce a new sanctions package against Iran that Washington has described as its toughest financial offensive yet. Iran has responded by warning that it could halt Gulf oil exports if economic pressure intensifies. Brent crude closed Friday at $94.39 a barrel and traded around $93.45 early Monday as markets positioned for the announcement.

The conflict is now approaching six months, and the Strait of Hormuz remains severely constrained. Iran’s control over passage and selective permissions for some vessels have turned shipping access into a direct instrument of pressure on global energy markets.

Philippine implication

For an energy-importing country already facing 6.2% inflation, another sustained oil spike would hit transport, freight, aviation, the peso and household purchasing power at the same time. That is why Hormuz is now part of the Philippine inflation story.

Technology · AI + startups

Tomorrow’s Philippine AI test is not whether founders can use the tools. It is whether they can build economics around them.

Echelon Philippines 2026 opens August 25 at SMX Aura under the theme “Age of the AIpreneur.” Its agenda includes government and startup leaders discussing policy, infrastructure, research, talent, commercialization, fundraising and practical AI adoption.

The timing is important because the AI industry’s physical economics are becoming harder to ignore. Data centers, memory, power and high-end servers are absorbing enormous capital globally. That makes the Philippine opportunity less about reproducing frontier models and more about finding defensible local use cases with clear paying customers.

FMB read

The strongest Philippine AI companies will not win by attaching “AI” to an ordinary product. They will win by eliminating an expensive problem, owning useful data or distribution, and proving that the automation improves margins or customer outcomes.

Climate · Food + water security

El Niño is moving from forecast to planning problem.

PAGASA says El Niño is present in the tropical Pacific and is likely to intensify and persist through the first half of 2027. Its outlook assigns a high probability to a very strong event in late 2026. Reuters reported that some international forecasters now see a possibility that the developing event could become the strongest on record.

For the Philippines, El Niño does not mean every place becomes dry immediately. Western areas can still receive heavy Habagat rainfall while other regions face below-normal rainfall, drought and crop stress. That combination makes water management, agriculture and local preparedness more complicated, not less.

Why it matters

A very strong El Niño can become an inflation story through food, electricity and water. It can also become a tourism, health and local-government story. The correct response starts before reservoirs, farms and households are already under stress.

Sources:DOST-PAGASA — ENSO monitoringReuters — global El Niño outlookReuters — economic effects

The signal in one sentence

Policy makers are being forced to manage several kinds of scarcity at once: affordable money, secure energy, computing capacity and reliable water.

Side digest

Taiwan: President Lai Ching-te used the anniversary of the 1958 Taiwan Strait crisis to argue that peace must be secured through stronger defence, while Taiwan plans defence spending above NT$1 trillion in 2027. Reuters reported the remarks from Kinmen on August 23.

Creative industries: President Marcos has directed national agencies to support Philippine Creative Industries Month in September under the theme “We Are a Creative Nation.” The policy framework is real; the test is whether institutions turn it into products, jobs, IP and exportable work rather than ceremonial content.

Tourism: The Department of Tourism’s Discover More to Love platform is actively packaging destinations, events and travel deals, including music-fandom-led offers. The stronger strategic question is conversion: whether cultural attention produces measurable travel demand.

Media: No new media-industry development reviewed this morning outranks the continuing issue of platform responsibility and graphic violent content after the Zamboanga school attack.

What FMB should watch

This week’s pressure points are unusually concentrated.

BSP, August 27: watch the rate move and the language on inflation, oil and growth.

Iran sanctions: watch whether Washington’s package materially changes Chinese, Gulf or other trade with Tehran and whether Iran follows through on threats against oil exports.

Hormuz: actual vessel traffic remains a better operational indicator than political statements.

Echelon Philippines: look for startups that can explain who pays, what problem disappears, and what advantage survives when everyone has access to similar AI models.

El Niño: watch PAGASA’s next advisory, drought assessment, reservoir conditions and food-price sensitivity.

Duterte impeachment: watch whether the revised schedule actually accelerates proceedings without creating new procedural disputes.

FMB bottom line

Scarcity is setting the agenda.

The Philippines is managing expensive money while growth is weak. The world is managing expensive energy through a constrained maritime corridor. AI companies are discovering that intelligence still depends on costly physical infrastructure. Climate agencies are warning that water and food security could come under greater pressure as El Niño intensifies.

The useful question today is not which headline is loudest. It is which constraint will force the next major decision.
